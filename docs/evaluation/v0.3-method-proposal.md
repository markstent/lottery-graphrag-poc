# Method v0.3 proposal (CP4)

This page proposes a changed method for a second official run, reported as v0.3.0.
The project owner replied "approve and continue" on 2026-10-27, which approves this page and the recommendations in section 6 and opens the CP4 build.
It does not approve the frozen method, the go-ahead for Neo4j or the official run; those come at the stops in section 7.
Decision numbers start at D-083, because D-082 is taken by Help v2.
The official run v0.2.0 stays the record of the old method, and nothing in it is rewritten.

## 1. Summary for the owner

The first official run (v0.2.0) found no detectable difference between text search (A), the graph (B) and the graph with the ontology (C).
Looking into its data shows two things that matter more than the headline.

First, the way evidence was chosen held the graph back.
Every one of the 1,350 prompts held exactly 12 items, and none came near the 2,000-token limit (the largest used 1,489).
So the 12-item limit decided what the model saw, and the graph's records lost to text that a text-ranking model preferred.
Half of all answers (654 of 1,348 judged) said the sources did not contain the answer.

Second, the two ways of marking an answer right were each wrong in a specific, fixable way.
The automatic marker scored correct dates as wrong when the answer added a time ("2025-10-07T00:00:00"), which cost all three approaches on one question type.
The model judge often marked "the sources do not say" as a fully correct answer, even when a real answer existed.
Without those two faults, and counting only answers that gave an answer, the two measures agree on 83 percent of answers, not 53 percent.

The proposal is to:

1. let the graph rank its own records by how they connect to the question, while text search keeps its own ranking;
2. fill the same token budget for every approach instead of 12 items;
3. show each record to the model as short facts with their source, and show for graph records how they connect to the question;
4. fix both markers, add a measure of whether answers are backed by the evidence they cite, and run on more, unseen worlds.

To guard against the charge that the method was tuned until the graph won, all development happens on the presentation world only, every rule and value is written down before measuring, and the official comparison runs on worlds no one has looked at.
The new result is published as v0.3.0 whatever it shows, including no difference.

## 2. What the v0.2.0 data shows

All figures below come from `data/evaluation/runs/official-2026-10-26/results.json` (1,350 answers, 1,348 judged), read with short scripts and no model.
They are analysis of the old run, not new results.

### 2.1 The 12 slots bound; the token budget did not

| Approach | Prompts with 12 items | Mean tokens | 90th percentile | Largest |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 450 of 450 | 1,150 | 1,431 | 1,489 |
| B | 450 of 450 | 1,130 | 1,375 | 1,467 |
| C | 450 of 450 | 1,107 | 1,383 | 1,467 |

Tokens are the project's estimate (`estimate_tokens` in `backend/app/retrieval/standard/context.py`, four characters per token).
A block averages 375 characters (about 94 tokens), so the 2,000-token budget would hold about 21 blocks.
The selection diagnostic of 2026-10-25 (`data/evaluation/diagnostics/20261025T200934Z-selection/`) found every claim the graph found and the prompt left out was refused as `slot_limit`: 23 claims for B and 51 for C, none for the budget.

### 2.2 How evidence is selected today

- A (`StandardRagPipeline.run`, `backend/app/retrieval/standard/pipeline.py`): hybrid search returns the top 40 (`RAG_TOP_K`), the shared cross-encoder `BAAI/bge-reranker-v2-m3` re-ranks them (`backend/app/retrieval/standard/reranking.py`), and `assemble_context` keeps items in that order until 12 slots or 2,000 tokens are used.
- B and C (`backend/app/retrieval/graph/stages.py`): `rank_and_scope` scores every node the bounded walk reached with the graph's composite score (`score_nodes` in `backend/app/retrieval/graph/ranking.py`), `select_and_attach` turns each node into a candidate shown on A's record block for the same row, then `assemble_and_generate` puts graph and document candidates through the same cross-encoder and `assemble_context`.
- The graph share (D-081): `select_context` and `_graph_slot_holders` in `context.py` give the first 6 usable nodes in the cross-encoder's order a graph slot (`RAG_GRAPH_SLOTS=6` of `RAG_EVIDENCE_SLOTS=12`, in `ContextSettings`, `backend/app/config/__init__.py`); unused graph slots go to documents.
- The graph's composite score already exists and is recorded with every candidate, but since D-025 it only orders candidates before the cross-encoder re-sorts them, so it never decides what reaches the prompt.
  Its four parts, with weights fixed since before v0.2.0 (`DEFAULT_RANKING_WEIGHTS`): distance from the seed, `1 / (1 + hops / 2)`, weight 0.25; the weakest relationship on the path from the fixed table `RELATIONSHIP_WEIGHTS` in `backend/app/retrieval/graph/traversal.py`, weight 0.25; cosine of question and node text with the shared embedding model, weight 0.35; whether the record is still valid on the reference date, weight 0.15.
- Nothing today works out what the question asks for (a platform version, a market, a mechanic).
  The resolvers in `backend/app/retrieval/graph/resolution.py` find the entities a question names (ids, market names, ontology terms), not the type of answer it wants.

### 2.3 How graph records reach the prompt today

A graph node is shown on exactly the record block A indexes for the same row (D-025, D-028; `SourceLookup.node_text` in `backend/app/retrieval/graph/source_lookup.py`), rendered by `context_block` and `build_user_prompt` in `backend/app/retrieval/standard/generation.py` as `[n] <title> (<market>, <language>)` followed by the text.
Nothing tells the model how a record connects to the question.
A real example: C's prompt for `L05-2026-INC_037`, "Which other markets had incidents on the same platform version as incident INC_037?", repeat 0.
Its first two of twelve blocks were:

```text

Incident INC_037 in United Kingdom: Identity service latency causing timeouts. Seen on platform version 2024.2.0 (PLV_IDENTITY_2). Type Sign-in outage (ITYPE_UK_SIGN_IN). Severity MEDIUM. Platform Identity Service (PLT_IDENTITY). Platform version 2024.2.0 (PLV_IDENTITY_2). Affected games: SwiftSix (GAME_UK_06). Started 2025-02-07T01:59:28. Resolved 2025-02-07T02:51:28. Root cause RC_27. Source: incident_management table incidents, record INC_037.

[2] support_cases: SUP_00121 (...)
Support case SUP_00121 in United Kingdom: Synthetic support contact describing a player-reported issue. Category Game or rules query (SUPC_UK_GAME). Game Albion Millions (GAME_UK_01). Incident INC_037. Opened 2025-02-20T10:12:09. Closed 2025-02-27T02:12:09. Sentiment score -0.205. Source: support_platform table support_cases, record SUP_00121.
```

Blocks 3 to 5 were three more support cases of INC_037, and blocks 6 to 12 were incidents on other platform versions.
The record that answers the question, INC_024 (the Czech Republic incident on the same version `PLV_IDENTITY_2`), was not in the prompt; the diagnostic put it at cross-encoder rank 32 in C and 39 in B.
The answer said the sources did not contain the information.
Two things are visible here: records already carry their source ("Source: ... record INC_037"), so provenance is present, but the relation the graph walked (INC_037 to PLV_IDENTITY_2 to INC_024) is not shown, and the record that holds the answer did not get in.

### 2.4 Why the judge and the automatic answer score disagree

The report's agreement figure (`judge_pairs` and `agreement` in `backend/app/evaluation/judge.py`) compares "deterministic answer score at least 0.5" with "judge correctness 2".
What each measures:

- The deterministic answer score (`answer_score` and `check_score` in `backend/app/evaluation/checks.py`) is the mean of text checks from the answer key: a number, a token (an id or date), a set of ids with F1, or a yes/no verdict.
  It cannot tell an abstention from a wrong answer, and it is only as good as its matching rules.
- The judge (`ollama/gemma4:31b`, prompt 1.0.0, `JUDGE_SYSTEM_PROMPT`) scores correctness 0, 1 or 2 as "the answer agrees with the reference answer", seeing the reference answer, the key facts, the shown blocks and the answer.

Where the 635 disagreements come from:

| Cause | Answers | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Judge marks an abstention as correct | 290 | The answer says the sources do not answer; the judge gives correctness 2 although the reference answer gives one (all of L11, most of L06, L07, L12) |
| Automatic date check misses a time suffix | 78 | The answer states the right date as `2025-10-07T00:00:00`, copied from the record block (`_render` in `backend/app/retrieval/standard/records.py` prints datetimes in ISO form); the token check's word boundary (`word_pattern` in `backend/app/evaluation/claims.py`) fails on the "T" that follows; every L03 answer had the right date |
| Answer names a version label instead of its id | 17 | L02 answers "2024.3.0" where the key wants `PLV_WEB_3`; the judge accepts it, though eight platform versions share that label |
| Other answers scored below 0.5 and judged correct | 23 | Set questions L10, L13, L14 |
| Partial answers counted as right by the threshold | 190 | A set answer scoring between 0.5 and 0.92 passes the 0.5 threshold, while the judge mostly gives 1 ("partly"); this is a mismatch in the agreement definition, not an error in either measure |
| Answers scored 1 and judged below 2 | 37 | Mostly judged "partly" (31) |

Agreement by slice:

- By question type it ranges from 0.00 (L11, every answer an abstention) and 0.04 (L03, the date fault) to 1.00 (L01, L04, L17).
- By pipeline: A 0.58, B 0.50, C 0.52.
- Among the 613 answers that gave an answer, outside L03, "score exactly 1" against "judge 2" agrees on 0.83 (kappa 0.66), which is substantial agreement.
- The Pearson correlation of the two scores over all judged answers is 0.22.

The judge's leniency on abstentions is not neutral between approaches: it marked 97 of A's 207 abstentions correct, 108 of B's 225 and 124 of C's 222.
This alone lifts C's judge correctness relative to A's.

Likely causes:

1. The judge rubric lets "correctly said the context does not answer" (its abstention dimension) leak into correctness.
2. The automatic token check treats a date followed by a time as a different token.
3. The agreement statistic compares a three-level judge with a 0.5 cut of a continuous score.

So the controller's plan to hand-label about 100 answers "to find which measure is wrong" can be narrower: the data already names the faults.
The hand-labelled sample is still worth doing, to confirm the fixes and to measure the corrected agreement on answers nobody has looked at (section 4.4).

Illustration only, not a result: with the date check fixed, the H3 answer score of v0.2.0 would be 0.94 for A, 0.92 for B and 0.86 for C (reported 0.72, 0.67, 0.61), and the overall answer score 0.49, 0.50 and 0.50 (reported 0.44 for each).
The difference between approaches on H3 shrinks from 5.6 to about 3 points for B against A, because A happened to write the date without a time 3 times.

### 2.5 What groundedness checks exist today

- `parse_answer` (`generation.py`) keeps only citations to blocks the model was shown.
- `citation_metrics` (`checks.py`) gives citation accuracy, the share of cited blocks that carry a claim from the answer key, and the numeric citation rate, the share of sentences with a number that carry a citation.
  Neither checks that a cited block supports the sentence that cites it.
- The judge scores faithfulness and citation accuracy (0 to 2); both were close to 2 for every approach in v0.2.0 (1.91 to 1.96 overall), so they separate little.

A first sketch on v0.2.0 shows a deterministic check is feasible: of the record ids an answer states that are not in the question, 5.7 percent (A), 8.6 percent (B) and 5.6 percent (C) appear in no block the model was shown, and 15.6, 12.6 and 9.2 percent appear in none of the blocks the answer cites.
These numbers are a sketch to show the check works, not a measure; section 4.5 defines the measure.

### 2.6 Other observations

- 26 percent of verdicts are a status word ("success" 204, "ok" 81, "known" 70) instead of a one-line answer.
  The prompt's verdict instruction (`SYSTEM_PROMPT`, prompt 2.0.0) is open to that reading.
  It only matters for yes/no questions (L04 still agrees 1.00) and for detecting abstentions, which use "unknown" and work.
- Abstentions by stratum and approach: H1 64, 64 and 58 percent; H2 57, 68 and 61 percent; H3 0, 3 and 8 percent; H5 63, 63 and 71 percent (A, B, C).

## 3. Guards against tuning until the graph wins

These apply to every change below.

1. Development uses the presentation world (seed 2026) only.
   Its bank, diagnostic and subset runs are development evidence and are never reported as a result of v0.3.0.
2. The official run uses worlds no one has generated or looked at: seeds 2029 onwards.
   Seeds 2027 and 2028 were in v0.2.0, so they are seen and are not used.
3. Before any official measurement, one decision record per change fixes every rule and value in writing, together with the tested comparisons, the metrics, the seeds, the models and the repeats.
   The record names the commit of the frozen code; the run refuses a dirty tree, as it does today.
4. No value is chosen by trying values against seeds 2029 onwards.
   If the official run fails mechanically it is resumed or rerun with the same settings; if it succeeds and shows no difference, that is the result.
5. v0.2.0 stays as published.
   The rescoring of v0.2.0 under the fixed scorer, if the owner wants it, is a labelled addendum, not a replacement.
6. The method change is declared wherever v0.3.0 is reported: it was made after seeing v0.2.0, with the reasons in this page.

## 4. The changes

Each change states what changes, fairness, expected effect, risk, the values to fix before measuring, and how it is tested.

### 4.1 Change 1: the graph ranks its own records

**What changes.**
In B and C the cross-encoder no longer orders graph candidates; it still orders the document channel exactly as in A.
Graph candidates are ordered by a graph relevance rule built from the walk's own information:

1. the seeds the question resolved to come first;
2. then nodes that hold what the question asks for (the "target type", below);
3. then every other node;
4. within each tier, the existing composite score with its existing weights, then node id for determinism.

The target type is found from the question text by matching the graph schema's own vocabulary: node label names split into words (`PlatformVersion` as "platform version", `GameMechanic` as "game mechanic" and "mechanic") and the table names of `NODE_SOURCES`.
A node holds the target type when its label is that type, or when the walk reached it with a relationship to a node of that type that its record states (for example an Incident with `AFFECTS_MARKET` when the question asks for markets).
In C the ontology's concept labels and synonyms may also name a target type; in B only the schema words do, so the ontology's contribution stays in C.

Files and functions:

- `backend/app/retrieval/graph/ranking.py`: a new `graph_relevance_order` beside `score_nodes`, which stays as the within-tier score.
- `backend/app/retrieval/graph/resolution.py`: a new deterministic target-type matcher (the vocabulary comes from the schema and the ontology, never from the bank).
- `backend/app/retrieval/graph/stages.py`: `select_and_attach` orders candidates by the new rule; `assemble_and_generate` re-ranks only `documents.documents` with the cross-encoder and hands both ordered lists to the new selection (change 2); `_node_outcomes` records the graph rank instead of a cross-encoder score for nodes.
- `backend/app/events/payloads.py` and `backend/app/api/event_schemas.py`: node events carry the tier and graph rank; the Live Lab's selection column (`frontend/src/screens/live-lab/columns.ts`) shows them.
- `backend/app/evaluation/diagnostics.py`: report each claim record's graph rank, so the diagnostic measures reach under the new rule.

**Fairness.**
A keeps its tuned hybrid search and cross-encoder unchanged.
Each approach now ranks with the information it has: text relevance for documents, connection to the question for graph records.
The target-type matcher is a cue that A does not get.
That is a declared difference: it uses the schema, which is part of what the graph and ontology layers add, but a reader could argue that a text pipeline could also use a table filter.
The alternative (giving A a boost for record blocks of the target table) would change A's tuned baseline, which the owner asked to keep.

**Expected effect.**
More of the graph's claim records reach the prompt in B and C; the diagnostic found the graph reaches 55 (B) and 88 (C) of 122 claims on seed 2026, against 41 and 43 in the prompt.
How many reach the prompt under the new rule is unknown until built.
On the L05 example, INC_024 is most likely two hops from the seed, over two `RELATED_TO_VERSION` edges (weight 0.9), while the anchor's support cases are one hop away, so whether it reaches the prompt depends on the composite score within the tier.

**Risk.**
A hand-built ordering is open to the charge that it was shaped on the bank; the guard is section 3, and the rule has one new binary tier and no new continuous weight.
If the target-type matcher fires on common words ("market" appears in many questions), tier 2 may hold most nodes and add little.
The graph may rank well on reach and still pick records the answer does not need, in which case B and C score no better.

**Values to fix before measuring.**

| Value | Recommended | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Tier order | seeds, target-type holders, others | Seeds are the question's subject; one binary tier keeps the free choices few |
| Within-tier score | `score_nodes` with `DEFAULT_RANKING_WEIGHTS` unchanged | Recorded with every run since before v0.2.0, not tuned on results |
| Fallback seed (D-027) | counts as a seed | It is the node the walk started from |
| Target-type vocabulary | schema label words and table names; in C also concept labels and synonyms | Comes from the schema and ontology, not from questions |
| Traversal limits | unchanged (depth 2, 100 per hop, 4 per type, 200 nodes) | Keeps reach equal to v0.2.0, so the change isolates ranking |

**Tests.**
Unit tests of the tier and tie-break order on fixed traversals; a test that the matcher reads only schema and ontology inputs; the existing truth-ledger isolation tests extended to the new module; a test that A's selection is byte-identical to today's for the same candidates when the budget is the same; a deterministic CI run with the lexical re-ranker and fake models.

### 4.2 Change 2: one token budget, no item limit

**What changes.**
`evidence_slots` and `graph_slots` go; every approach fills the same token budget.
A walks its cross-encoder list as today until the budget is full.
B and C walk two ranked lists, documents (cross-encoder order) and graph records (change 1 order), taking one from each in turn, starting with the document channel, until no candidate fits.
A record that appears in both lists (a node and its record block) is taken once, at its first appearance.
The near-duplicate check on document chunks stays (Jaccard 0.85).
When one list is used up, the other continues alone.

Files and functions:

- `backend/app/retrieval/standard/context.py`: `select_context` loses `max_items` and `graph_slots`; `_graph_slot_holders` is removed; a new `interleave_by_budget` (or a parameter of `select_context`) merges two ranked lists; `SELECTION_SLOT_LIMIT` is no longer produced.
- `backend/app/config/__init__.py`: `ContextSettings.evidence_slots` and `graph_slots`, `RAG_EVIDENCE_SLOTS` and `RAG_GRAPH_SLOTS` are removed; `SELECTION_RULE` becomes a new label (for example `graph-rank-interleave-budget`), so `compare` and resume never pair v0.3 with v0.2 runs.
- `backend/app/evaluation/runner.py` (`settings_fields`), `backend/app/evaluation/diagnostics.py`, `backend/app/retrieval/standard/pipeline.py` and `stages.py`: drop slot fields from records and events.
- `.env.example`, `docs/retrieval.md`, `docs/operations.md`: the settings change.
- Frontend: the slot count in the Live Lab and the recorded fixtures under `frontend/src/run/fixtures/`.

**Fairness.**
The budget is the same for every approach, and the merge has no free share: each channel offers its best remaining item in turn.
This is parameter-free in the sense that matters (no share value to choose), unlike the half share of D-081.
A gains as much room as B and C.

**Expected effect.**
Prompts grow from about 12 items and 1,130 tokens to about 21 items and close to 2,000 tokens for every approach.
A may improve too, since items ranked 13 to 21 enter; that is intended, since the question is what the graph adds over a well-provisioned A.
Evidence precision, as scored today (`evidence_score` in `backend/app/evaluation/scoring.py`: the share of selected items that carry a claim), will fall for everyone because more items are shown; see decision 7.

**Risk.**
Longer prompts can bury the answer in the middle; a small answer model may suffer more.
Answers take somewhat longer.
The single-pass walk keeps its known limitation (a refused item is not revisited).

**Values to fix before measuring.**

| Value | Recommended | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Budget | 2,000 tokens, unchanged | It never bound in v0.2.0, so removing the item limit alone lets every approach use about 70 percent more context; at 4,000 A would fit almost its whole 40-item pool (about 3,760 tokens), and its ranking would stop mattering |
| Who starts the alternation | the document channel | Then B's and C's first block is A's first block, and differences come from what the graph adds |
| Document pool | top 40 (`RAG_TOP_K`), unchanged | A's tuned setting |
| Token estimate | four characters per token, unchanged | Equal for all; a real tokeniser would change every count |

**Tests.**
Unit tests of the merge (alternation, exhaustion of one list, duplicates across lists, budget refusal); a property test that no selection exceeds the budget; a test that A's selection equals its cross-encoder order cut at the budget.

### 4.3 Change 3: records as compact facts; graph records with their path

**What changes.**
In the prompt, every structured record, in every approach, is shown as compact facts with its source, grouped under the entity:

```text
[3] Incident INC_024 (source: incident_management.incidents, record INC_024)
- market: MKT_CZ (Czech Republic)
- platform version: PLV_IDENTITY_2 (2024.2.0)
- severity: MEDIUM; started 2025-08-19; resolved 2025-08-20
- summary: Identity service latency causing timeouts.
- reached from INC_037: INC_037 -RELATED_TO_VERSION-> PLV_IDENTITY_2 <-RELATED_TO_VERSION- INC_024
```

The facts come from the same row A indexes (D-028 holds: no column A could not index).
The last line, the path the walk took from a seed, appears only for graph records in B and C: it is the graph's product.
Document chunks stay prose.
The indexed text, the embeddings and the cross-encoder's input do not change, so retrieval is untouched; only what the answer model reads changes.
The prompt also clarifies that the verdict is the one-line answer, not a status (section 2.6).

Files and functions:

- `backend/app/retrieval/standard/records.py`: a fact serialiser per table beside `SERIALISERS`, sharing the column choices; dates printed without a midnight time.
- `backend/app/retrieval/standard/generation.py`: `context_block` renders a record by its facts and a graph record with its path; `SYSTEM_PROMPT` and `PROMPT_VERSION` 3.0.0; `citations_for` unchanged (it already carries table and record id).
- `backend/app/retrieval/graph/stages.py`: `_structured_chunk` carries the path (`TraversalResult.path_to`, already on `ScoredNode.path`) with relationship names.
- `backend/app/evaluation/judge.py`: the judge sees the same blocks (section 4.4 on blinding).

**Fairness.**
The format is the same for the same record in A, B and C, so format alone cannot favour the graph.
The path line is a B- and C-only addition to the prompt, which D-025 ruled out; it is proposed on purpose, because how a record connects to the question is what a graph adds.
Its tokens count against the same budget.
It also gives the judge a tell: a block with a path line comes from B or C.
The judge is not primary (D-031), and the limitation is declared.

**Expected effect.**
Shorter records let more items fit the budget for every approach.
The path line should help multi-hop and cross-market answers (H1, H2) if the model uses it.
Citations to exact records are already possible today; the grouping makes it easier for the model to cite the record that holds a fact.

**Risk.**
A small model may read fact lists worse than prose.
Grouping by entity changes block sizes, which changes how many items fit.
The path line could mislead when the walk took a weak path.

**Values to fix before measuring.**

| Value | Recommended | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Fact format | as above, one line per column of the existing serialiser | Same content as the record block, less filler |
| Path | the discovery path from the nearest seed, one per record | The walk already records it; showing all paths would be unbounded |
| Path for seeds | none | A seed is the question's own entity |
| Applies to A | yes, the same fact format for every record | Keeps format equal; recommended over facts for B and C only |
| Prompt version | 3.0.0 | Any prompt change is recorded |

**Tests.**
Golden tests of the rendered block per table; a test that the same row renders the same facts in A, B and C apart from the path line; a test that fact text holds no column absent from the record block; the prompt hash test.

### 4.4 Change 4a: fix the two answer measures

**What changes.**

1. Scorer 2.1.0 (`backend/app/evaluation/checks.py`): a date token check also matches the same date followed by a time (`T00:00:00`, with or without seconds), and nothing else.
2. Judge prompt 1.1.0 (`JUDGE_SYSTEM_PROMPT`): correctness is 0 when the answer says the sources do not answer and the reference answer gives an answer; the abstention dimension stays as it is.
3. Agreement (`judge_pairs`, `AGREEMENT_ANSWER_THRESHOLD`, `_judge_agreement` in `backend/app/evaluation/report.py`): report three-level agreement (score 0, between 0 and 1, exactly 1 against judge 0, 1, 2) and weighted kappa, separately for answered and abstaining answers.
4. A hand-labelled sample to confirm the fixes, built before the v0.3.0 run from v0.2.0 answers: about 100 answers, stratified over the disagreement cells of section 2.4 (25 judged-correct abstentions, 15 L03 dates, 10 version-label answers, 25 partial set answers, 25 where the measures agree), sampled by a hashed seed.
   The labeller sees the question, reference answer, shown blocks and answer, not the approach, and applies the judge's rubric with the abstention rule above.
   The labels live under `data/evaluation/` with the evaluator's files and never reach a pipeline.

**Fairness.**
The fixes apply to every approach.
The date fault cost B and C slightly more than A in v0.2.0 (A wrote the date without a time 3 times of 27); the judge's leniency favoured C.

**Expected effect.**
The two measures should agree on most answered questions, as they already do once the faults are removed (0.83).
The labelled sample says which measure to trust where they still differ.

**Risk.**
The version-label case (L02) has no clean rule: "2024.3.0" names eight versions, so the key's insistence on the id is defensible; the labels decide whether to add the label as an alias when it is unique within the incident's platform.
Set checks credit names mentioned in an abstention (L12 scored 0.33 on an abstention); whether an abstaining answer should score 0 on set checks is a question for the labels, not a fix proposed blind.

**Values to fix before measuring.**
The date rule, the judge wording, the agreement statistics, the sample size and strata, the labeller (recommended: the owner, or the owner and the controller labelling independently with disagreements discussed), and the rule for acting on the labels (recommended: a scorer or judge rule changes only where the labels show it wrong on at least 80 percent of its cell).

**Tests.**
Unit tests of the date rule (with and without time, a different date with the same prefix must not match); judge prompt hash; agreement statistics on fixed pairs.

### 4.5 Change 4b: a groundedness measure

**What changes.**
A deterministic, model-free measure in `backend/app/evaluation/checks.py`, next to `citation_metrics`, built on the existing `_sentences` and `scored_numbers`:

- An answer's checkable atoms are the ids, numbers and dates it states, outside citation markers and outside the question text.
- Supported share: atoms that appear in a block the sentence cites, over all atoms.
- Unsupported share (the hallucination signal): atoms that appear in no block the model was shown.
- Abstentions with no atoms are counted apart, not as fully grounded.

It is recorded per answer in `RunScore` (`runner.py`), summarised in `report.py`, and shown as a descriptive metric outside the Holm family in v0.3.0.
It can also be computed for v0.2.0 from `results.json`, since the answers and shown blocks are stored, as a labelled addendum.
The judge's faithfulness score stays beside it.

**Fairness.**
The same text check for every approach; it uses no answer key, so it measures support, not correctness.

**Expected effect.**
A real difference between approaches in how often answers state ids or numbers their evidence does not hold; the sketch of section 2.5 suggests C cites more precisely.

**Risk.**
It only sees ids, numbers and dates; a wrong claim in plain words is invisible to it.
Ids written differently ("INC 024") would count as unsupported; the matching must use the same normalisation as the scorer.

**Values to fix before measuring.**
The atom patterns (recommended: the scorer's own identifier, number and date patterns), whether a sentence without a citation inherits the next sentence's citation (recommended: no), and whether it joins the tested family (recommended: no, descriptive in v0.3.0, because it is new).

**Tests.**
Unit tests on hand-written answers and blocks; a test that it reads no answer key.

### 4.6 Change 4c (optional): a larger answer model (H7)

**What changes.**
After the local run, a second run with a hosted larger answer model, `Qwen3 235B A22B Instruct 2507` on OpenRouter, and the same local judge, paired with `compare_runs` (`backend/app/evaluation/official.py`), which already refuses two runs with different judges.

**Fairness.**
Same worlds, banks, method and judge; only the answer model differs.

**Expected effect.**
Shows whether a stronger reader uses the graph's evidence better.

**Risk.**
Needs `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` and a dry run to check the reasoning switch holds; prices must be checked on openrouter.ai before the run.

**Values to fix before measuring.**
The model, provider, temperature 0 and reasoning off, set now and unchanged.

**Tests.**
The existing four-question dry run with the hosted model.

### 4.7 Change 4d: more question groups

Statistical power comes from groups (a logical question in one world, D-032), not from anchors or repeats, because instances of one question in one world are correlated.
From v0.2.0's group-level differences, a rough minimum detectable difference (80 percent power, two-sided 5 percent, before the Holm correction) is:

| Stratum and metric | Groups in v0.2.0 | Detectable with 3 worlds | Detectable with 6 worlds |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 answer, B against A | 12 | 16 points | 11 points |
| H3 answer, B against A | 12 | 11 points | 7 points |
| H5 answer, B against A | 24 | 8 points | 5 points |
| H1 evidence F1, B against A | 12 | 2.3 points | 1.6 points |

These are estimates from a normal approximation on v0.2.0's spread; the new method will change the spread.
Two ways to add groups:

1. More unseen worlds (recommended): six, seeds 2029 to 2034, doubling every stratum's groups.
   No code change: `make run-benchmark SEEDS=2029,2030,2031,2032,2033,2034`.
2. More question templates (bank 2.1.0): adds groups too, but writing new questions after seeing results invites the charge of choosing graph-friendly questions, and needs new grounding code.
   Not recommended for v0.3.0.

## 5. Generating and ingesting unseen worlds

- `run_benchmark` in `backend/app/evaluation/official.py` prepares each seed with `prepare_world`: it generates the world and its truth ledger when missing (`data/generated/dev-seed<seed>/`, `data/truth/dev-seed<seed>.json`), wipes Neo4j and ingests the world's graph, indexes a Qdrant collection `semantic_context_lab_seed<seed>`, and grounds the frozen bank (`data/evaluation/bank/bank-2.0.0-dev-seed<seed>.json`).
- Seed 2028 took about 4 minutes from generation to a finished index on 2026-10-19 (manifest `generated_at` 17:16:13, index `indexed_at` 17:20:35); allow about 5 minutes per world.
- Neo4j holds one world at a time, so every world wipes the graph, and after the last world the presentation world (seed 2026) is ingested again (`_presentation_graph_restored`).
  The Live Lab is unavailable for the run's duration (graph lease, D-037).
  The graph holds 36,888 nodes today (read-only count, 2026-10-27); the run must leave it at that count.
- This changes the live database and so needs the owner's go-ahead, as the dry run and the v0.2.0 run did.
- Generating a world early, without ingesting it, would also create its truth ledger; to keep the worlds unseen, nobody inspects their data or banks before the run, and the run generates them itself.

## 9. What this can and cannot show

- It can show whether the graph and the ontology add measurable value when each approach ranks its own evidence and all three fill the same budget, on worlds the method was not developed on.
- It cannot show that v0.2.0 was wrong: v0.2.0 measured its own method correctly, and both results stand side by side.
- The method was changed after seeing a result in which the graph did not win.
  That is declared, and the guards in section 3 limit what the change could have been fitted to, but they do not remove the doubt; a reader should weigh v0.3.0 with that in mind.
- The result may still show no difference.
  The graph may reach the facts and the small answer model may still not use them, or the bank's questions may be answerable well enough from text.
  If so, v0.3.0 reports no difference, and the next step (ontology-guided structured queries for C, with a matching text-to-SQL path for A) is a separate proposal.
- Six worlds of one synthetic enterprise, one small answer model and one judge do not generalise to other data, other models or real enterprises.
- The judge sees a tell (the path line) and stays secondary to the deterministic scores.
- Some changes favour B and C by design (the target-type cue and the path line are graph information); the result measures the graph with them, not a graph without them.
